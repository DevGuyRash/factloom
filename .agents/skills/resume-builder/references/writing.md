# Writing the resume

Write the strongest truthful case for the target's actual work. Use the research-to-evidence comparison to choose what leads, what to retain for searchability, and what this variant can omit. Explain consequential choices briefly; the person should review a useful draft, not make every editorial decision.

## Readers and length

Recruiters, hiring managers, parsers, search filters, and sometimes matching or screening models may see different versions of the material. Their behavior depends on the employer and system; a simulated AI review cannot predict a decision. Make the first part of the page communicate the target, level, and strongest proof, with readable evidence and accurate chronology throughout.

Choose the document type and length after reviewing all available context and source material and inviting extra notes. A concise resume, long CV, project-based record, or another requested format may need different depth and section order. Recommend a page limit or use judgment from the actual content, purpose, career stage, market, and destination requirements. Follow current official guidance when a regulated or market-specific format needs it.

Use editorial judgment to preserve the most meaningful content, especially achievements, professional development, distinctive responsibilities, and relevant projects. Set context-aware priorities rather than a fixed category ranking. Record the intended format, explicit must-keeps, and any useful preferences in the existing guide; reuse them across the batch and reconsider only when an output's audience or new evidence warrants it. When space creates a material choice, briefly recommend what to retain and ask what matters most. Offer optional 1–5 importance weights only if useful (5 means most important); these express preferences, not a scoring engine or permission to drop a must-keep. No category-by-category questionnaire is required.

Hard employer requirements and user must-keeps are constraints; page preferences and importance weights guide choices within them. If constraints conflict, explain the conflict and ask the smallest unresolved choice before finalizing. Where high-priority content competes with a preferred length, recommend more pages when the destination permits, or a specific condensation/omission for review. Inspect the real layout before cutting useful detail; available room can carry stronger evidence. Shorten repetition and lower-priority content before squeezing type and margins. The renderer's 9 pt floor is a mechanical limit, not a readability goal.

Use reverse chronology within experience, or a clear hybrid when relevant projects or earlier experience need prominence. Preserve dated roles and distinguish projects from employment. Set `pages` when a page limit is warranted; inspect the fitted output at normal reading size.

## Sections and variant boundaries

Use recognizable headings and text contact details in the document body: name, city or region, email, phone, and relevant authorized links. A headline describes the target argument without inventing an official title or seniority. Choose experience, projects, skills, education, credentials, and other sections according to the target.

When a summary is useful or requested, describe the professional identity the evidence supports, relevant breadth of work or settings, and a small number of concrete strengths demonstrated below. For a general resume, capture what carries across the person's plausible roles; retain a specialty as supporting evidence without making it their only identity. For an exact role, emphasize the overlap. Prefer specific capabilities to flattering adjectives. Every qualification, scope claim, and credential in the summary needs support; a desired next role is not experience already held.

For reusable resume and web/profile copies, use [named summary channels](../../../../docs/summaries.md) in `facts.yaml` and reference them from variants. Preserve the same supported professional core, not identical length or wording. Keep each copy grounded in primary fact references; do not treat authored synthesis as new evidence. Inspect and complete source-to-prose review before explicitly recording its digest. Never stamp review automatically as part of drafting, building or export; a recorded review is an assertion of review, not proof of truth or permission to publish.

Retain a concise skills section for relevant, supported skills, including recognizable names and abbreviations recruiters might search. Put the most important skills in context in experience or projects too; every item need not have its own bullet. Preserve the broader verified inventory in `facts.yaml` when trimming a variant.

Keep official employment titles, dates, and employment type intact. A reviewed functional descriptor can clarify an unfamiliar title without replacing it. Group contracts only when employers, clients, and dates remain intelligible and disclosure is permitted. Include gaps or personal context only when useful and chosen by the person; a complete resume does not require private explanations. Leave out unnecessary sensitive details.

Variants should make meaningfully different cases from the same facts. A teaching variant might emphasize instruction and learner progress; a skilled-trade variant might emphasize workmanship and safe practice; an administrative variant might emphasize coordination and accuracy. These are illustrative, not required categories: use this person’s actual target and evidence. Choose the mix from actual target requirements. If the argument and strongest evidence stay the same, reuse the variant and tailor it instead of creating another near-duplicate.

## Contributions and relevant value

A bullet should show the person's actual work and its relevant value: an object, method, decision, setting, people served, constraint, scale, deliverable, or consequence. Preserve the details that explain skill or distinguish the work while shortening repetition. Use ordinary verbs and one main idea, usually in one or two readable lines; vary the structure naturally. A stronger verb must still describe the same responsibility.

For example, confirmed duties of answering customer calls, scheduling service visits, and keeping spare-parts records can become “Scheduled service visits, answered customer calls, and maintained spare-parts records.” If a follow-up establishes that the person introduced a shared booking sheet and duplicate bookings stopped, “Introduced a shared booking sheet that eliminated duplicate service bookings” adds a supported qualitative achievement. A general summary can connect service coordination, customer communication, and recordkeeping. Research can suggest asking about dispatch software or turnaround time; answers must establish those details before use.

Connect the work to the people, organization, service, or purpose it supported. Preserve the forms of contribution relevant to that profession, including routine responsibilities whose scope, reliability, skill, or judgment provides strong evidence. Distinguish what was intended, what was delivered, what was used, and what was measured. Contributing to a team result does not establish sole ownership; completing training does not establish professional practice. Qualitative outcomes, concrete capabilities, and scope are useful when reliable numbers do not exist. Metrics need their units, period, baseline where relevant, attribution, and source; avoid implying causal gains from an estimate or team result.

Lead with supported achievements where available, then the responsibilities that establish relevant capability and accountability. A current or important role needs evidence beyond repeating the summary. Recover a meaningful example from its source or interview; if only the title is known, keep that limitation visible for review. Use workload to describe scope, not to imply quality or improvement; completion is not an increase, and an intended benefit is not a measured outcome.

Keep wording defensible in a follow-up conversation. Research can supply familiar language for a true claim, not missing experience. Name equivalent tools for what they actually are. Use supported keywords in readable context and in the skills section; term coverage is a diagnostic, not a target to maximize. Hidden text, copied unsupported requirements, and instructions to screening software have no place in the resume.

## Source edits and local delivery

Change `facts.yaml` or the variant, never generated files. Keep original source section/page and dated user-correction pointers in `evidence.md`; retain the fuller record when a variant is selective. Explain material omissions or combinations in the existing guide against the output's purpose; small editorial edits need no change log. Preserve approved factual scope through grammar, clarity, shortening, or reordering. Verify and, where not already approved, confirm changed meaning (titles, dates, qualifications, numbers, ownership, scope, use, or outcomes). Put unresolved sourced phrases in `confirm`, and non-bullet uncertainties in manual `review`. Research-inspired additions remain separate questions. Exact-wording approval still governs when requested. Reword bullets in place and append additions because existing specs reference their indexes.

Before introducing unresolved claims, inspect which variants actually select the changed bullets or fields. Add a specific manual `review` item and set `status: needs-review` only on those guides, or when a new intended use or an explicit review request needs approval. A shared file edit alone does not invalidate an unchanged sibling; purely editorial revisions retain existing approval. Use `./resumes build-resumes --person <slug> --variant <name> --out <review-dir>` while substantive approval is pending; approved changes can build directly into active with final file review. Review builds preserve active files but do not regenerate guide flags. Keep the established theme unless layout needs a change. For portals, an `ats: safe` flow theme is a starting point, not certification by an employer's parser.

Follow the destination's file type and size rules; otherwise provide a text-based PDF when available and the editable Word file. Local extraction checks only some content in a readable output, not every format or employer's field mapping. Inspect each deliverable's text and rendered pages as [review](review.md) describes. If PDF rendering or extraction is unavailable, deliver what was verified, name the missing check, and keep the unavailable format pending. Give the person the files, what each variant is for, and any exact claims needing their confirmation.

## Career-writing references

- [Harvard's resume guidance](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/) supports specific, active, factual language and authentic representation; its college-specific conventions are not universal requirements.
- [Berkeley's resume guidance](https://www.career.berkeley.edu/prepare-for-success/resumes/) connects relevant accomplishments and transferable skills with employer needs, quantifying when possible. [Amazon's recruiter guidance](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/amazon-job-application-resume-writing-tips) offers an employer perspective on role alignment and readable accomplishment detail. Neither makes an invented number necessary.
- [ACS's summary guidance](https://www.acs.org/careers/career-services/career-pathways/industry/resume.html) grounds summary claims in evidence later in the resume. Apply that principle without importing its chemistry-specific examples or personal-data advice into every profession.

These are contextual writing sources, not evidence of guaranteed selection or validation of an agent's behavior. Synthetic content follows the declared [research scope and controls](research-mode.md).
